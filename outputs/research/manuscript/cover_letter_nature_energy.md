# Cover letter

[Date]

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "Realising the power-system value of flexible AI computing: service constraints and price signals in China" for consideration as an Article in *Nature Energy*.

AI computing is the fastest-growing new electricity load in China and elsewhere, and field demonstrations have shown that AI clusters can modulate their power. Whether that technical flexibility becomes value for the power system is a separate question, and it is the one we answer. We connect three links that previous studies have treated separately: the service constraints under which computing tasks must still complete, the system conditions that give shifted energy any value, and the incentives that make an operator deliver what it technically could. The framework combines measured GPU power modes, production workload traces, a verified joint investment–dispatch model and firm behaviour under tariffs and contracts, and is applied to three Chinese provinces in a 2030 scenario built entirely from public data.

Three findings will interest the journal's readership. First, the value of flexibility is a property of the system, not of the load: it is below 1% of the incremental cost of serving AI load wherever interprovincial exchange is unconstrained and rises to several per cent, with gigawatts of avoided gas turbines, only where local scarcity exists. Second, firms optimising against current time-of-use tariff shapes realise only part of this value, and in one province the tariff valley misses the system's surplus hours entirely. Third, an event-based commitment contract adds almost nothing and is exposed to baseline manipulation, whereas a firm facing hourly prices shaped like the system's marginal cost comes within a few per cent of the coordinated optimum. The instrument that determines realised value is the shape of the price signal.

Relative to Colangelo et al. (*Nature Energy*, 2025), which demonstrated grid-interactive operation of an AI cluster, we quantify how much of that capability is realised under actual tariff structures and contracts. Relative to Senga, Wang and Knittel (*iScience*, 2026) and Chen and Zheng (ACM Sustainability Week, 2026), which model data-centre flexibility in capacity expansion, we add task-level deadlines drawn from production traces, measured idle power, firm behaviour and mechanism design. Relative to Zhang, Li and Wang (*Applied Energy*, 2025) on China's regional computing migration and Dunlap (2026) on resource adequacy, we replace exogenous flexibility assumptions with service-constrained schedules and test the incentive layer explicitly.

All data are public and are listed with sources, versions, checksums and licences in the Supplementary Information; the code and verification records are available in a repository that will be made public and archived with a DOI on publication. The manuscript states the evidence basis and limitations of every input, in particular that hourly provincial load shapes are reconstructions checked against published peaks. The work is not under consideration elsewhere, and the authors declare no competing interests. Should the editors consider the manuscript better suited to *Joule*, we would welcome a transfer.

Yours sincerely,
