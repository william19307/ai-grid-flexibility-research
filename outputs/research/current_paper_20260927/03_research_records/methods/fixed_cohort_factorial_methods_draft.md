# Controlled shape sensitivity — methods working text

To separate background shape from absolute compute scale, we construct a factorial diagnostic in which the electrical trajectories of each certified compute cohort are converted before background perturbations are applied. Within a fixed 192-hour window, the background at each node is transformed as B(alpha,t)=mean(B)+alpha[B(t)-mean(B)], for alpha in {0,0.5,1}. This transformation conserves window energy; it is not an annual load reconstruction or a statistically estimated uncertainty set.

The independent compute factor is an integer number of identical synchronous cluster replicas. At each replica level, all policy trajectories, original service certificates and the complete recovery horizon remain unchanged across background shapes. Full-active power and the idle ratio are explicit assumptions, independent of background energy or peak. The incremental interpretation of the cohort requires external evidence before empirical use. The NO_COHORT reference removes the entire represented cluster, including untargeted work and idle power.

We validate the input controls on three previously certified tariff pairings of one workload window and compare 72 policy solves against an analytic single-generator expansion model. This validates accounting and solver integration only. The background series is unvalidated, power conversion is assumed, and the generator and cost coefficients are artificial. These diagnostics do not establish provincial investment savings, measured service quality, annual reliability, or independent replication across three field sites.
