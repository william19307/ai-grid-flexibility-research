# Load observation qualification and sensitivity identification

Working revision text; the following does not certify a new provincial load trajectory.

Annual electricity matching does not validate the time profile or peak. The legacy series consists of hourly reconstructed shapes scaled to all-society annual electricity. Reported dispatch peaks may cover a different set of consumers and generators, losses and self-supply, and may be event-time rather than hourly averages. Before calibration we require matched year, geographic/electrical boundary, included demand categories, time resolution, timezone and event definition. A record from another year is retained as context, not promoted to a same-year calibration target. The sign of bias in a policy benefit cannot be inferred solely from the direction of a peak discrepancy.

Five records from primary regulator publications were qualified by their explicit periods and labels. None establishes the matching hourly all-society peak required for a direct correction. The Jiangsu January–November 2020 report gives a year-to-date dispatch maximum of 115,124.9 MW; the August event report locates an approximately 115,120 MW dispatch peak at 13:25 on 17 August. These are not independent measurements of an annual hourly-average target. The 2026 Guizhou report dates its 29,553 MW record to 4 January 2026, correcting the year-end 2025 label in the historical plausibility table.

The original peak sensitivity also changed the electrical scale of the AI cohort. The code first adjusted the background peak and then defined AI full-load power as a fixed fraction of that adjusted peak. In all eight paired historical scenarios, AI nameplate power and rigid AI energy fell together, by 10.26099% in Gansu and 7.21517% in Guizhou. Normalized task-pool hours were unchanged, which does not establish unchanged absolute workload or hardware. This is a joint load-shape/cohort-scale sensitivity, not an isolated test of robustness to background peak shape. Revised comparisons must hold the same certified cohort, absolute power mapping and service definition fixed while varying background inputs. Cohort size can be a separate factorial dimension.

The archived Jiangsu 2030 background is 753.748824 TWh, whereas the regulator reports 889.541 TWh of all-society consumption for 2025. The former is 15.2654% below the latter. This compares model background before separately added study AI with observed inclusive consumption; it is not an estimated forecast error of total scenario demand. The discrepancy requires an updated demand outlook and an explicit cohort inclusion/exclusion argument. A scenario below current observed consumption needs a stated, evidenced interpretation rather than automatic use as a present-day baseline.

The source/selection audit includes 14 date/value extraction checks, an independent decimal reconstruction of the three provincial annual growth calculations, and an eight-pair input audit. No old solver wrapper was executed, no historical result was overwritten, and no reported peak was used to fabricate a validated hourly trajectory. Primary source URLs and file hashes are preserved in the evidence package.
