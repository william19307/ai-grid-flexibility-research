# Working replacement: renewable-profile validation across temporal scales

Revision working text, 23 September 2026. This passage records a diagnostic and must not be inserted into the submitted manuscript as evidence that the provincial renewable inputs have been validated. The submitted files remain unchanged.

Annual energy calibration was distinguished from validation of the temporal generation profile. We first required a calibrated profile to respect nameplate capacity at every modelled interval. We then examined annual and quarterly discrepancies using a common observation boundary and retained all predefined conversion functions. Calibration parameters were estimated from the 2022 annual gross-generation total and held fixed for 2023 and 2024; no quarterly coefficients were fitted. Gross generation and grid exports were kept separate, and time-based turbine availability was not interpreted as an energy-weighted loss factor.

For an exploratory offshore diagnostic, we used the 2022–2024 controlled-offshore totals disclosed by Jiangsu New Energy Development Co., Ltd. The association with the 350 MW Rudong H2 project is supported by documentary evidence of full commissioning and the reported fleet boundary, but these totals are not a directly labelled plant-meter time series. Quarterly generation was obtained from cumulative first-quarter, half-year and nine-month disclosures and the annual total. Repeated 2023 disclosures were checked against the corresponding prior-year columns in the 2024 reports and agreed at their displayed precision. A cumulative quantity reported to 0.01 hundred-million kWh has a display-rounding half-width of 500 MWh; differences of two such quantities have a conservative half-width of 1,000 MWh. These bounds describe reporting precision, not measurement uncertainty. Consecutive quarterly differences share cumulative endpoints and are not statistically independent.

Let O_q and M_q denote reported and modelled quarterly gross energy. We report annual signed bias, B = sum_q(M_q − O_q)/sum_q O_q, together with a quarterly absolute-error measure, A = sum_q |M_q − O_q|/sum_q O_q. The descriptive cancellation fraction is 1 − |B|/A when A is positive. No significance test or confidence interval is inferred from the twelve quarters. All annual observations, annual model profiles and several cumulative observations were already known when the quarterly analysis plan was frozen; the exercise is therefore an exploratory diagnostic, not a blinded holdout.

The diagnostic demonstrates why annual agreement alone is insufficient. For the NREL reference shape in 2023, annual bias was −0.55%, whereas the quarterly absolute-error measure was 13.08%. Quarterly biases were +12.55%, +12.79%, −8.10% and −17.17%, so positive and negative residuals largely cancelled in the annual sum. The V112 reference shape likewise had annual bias of −1.34% but a quarterly absolute-error measure of 11.97%. Neither reference shape is established as the actual H2 turbine conversion. The generic conversion remained physically inadmissible because annual scaling produced interval outputs above nameplate capacity; it was retained only as a failed diagnostic comparator.

These findings do not establish the causes of the residuals, hourly generation accuracy or provincial representativeness. Manufacturer-specific equipment and power curves, operational losses and matched meter data are still required before revised renewable profiles can support the provincial capacity and reliability claims. Numerical agreement between two implementations is reported separately from agreement with observations.
