# Working replacement: information parity and event procurement

Revision working text, 23 September 2026. This is a mathematical framework and a specification for the remaining empirical comparison. It must not be described as a completed provincial mechanism experiment.

The price and event benchmarks must share the same decision-time information, physical task model, service requirements and outside option. A planning function accepts forecast marginal resource costs and cannot receive realized costs. A separate evaluator operates on frozen trajectories without reselecting procurement quantities or rescheduling jobs. This software separation is necessary but does not establish that a supplied forecast was available at decision time; empirical forecasts must be accompanied by their issue times, vintages and input provenance. The present implementation assumes a fully known set of preemptive, divisible tasks and is not an online gang scheduler.

For an explicit finite offer set K containing zero, a positive event commitment k requires a reduction of at least k in every event interval relative to an immutable outside-option schedule. The firm minimizes its tariff bill plus stipulated service costs subject to the same work, deadline and physical capacity constraints. An optional contract term limits aggregate non-event recovery energy relative to the outside option. Zero procurement is a genuine opt-out and preserves the outside schedule. We retain infeasible offers rather than repairing job deadlines.

Let B(x) be the firm's tariff bill plus service cost and F_k the feasible schedules under commitment k. Given B*_k = min_{x in F_k} B(x), we bound forecast resource cost over {x in F_k: B(x) <= B*_k + epsilon}. The reported epsilon is a numerical allowance, not an empirical indifference range. Procurement maximizes forecast savings under the worst admissible follower response, with smaller commitments preferred when values are equal within the reported selection tolerance. Optimality is restricted to the supplied offer set; no continuous-quantity global optimum or strategic equilibrium is claimed.

Electricity bills and participation payments are transfers. For supply resource cost G, service cost V, electricity bill T and participation transfer Pi, the resource saving relative to the outside option is G_0 - G_x + V_0 - V_x. The retailer's cash improvement is G_0 - G_x + T_x - T_0 - Pi, and the provider's welfare change is Pi - (T_x - T_0) - (V_x - V_0). Their sum equals the resource saving. The conditional weak-participation floor max(0, T_x + V_x - T_0 - V_0) is an accounting quantity under known provider costs; it is not an incentive-compatible reimbursement rule.

The current price benchmark posts an exogenous forecast marginal-resource-cost vector. Under linear supply costs and identical service valuation, its objective coincides with the forecast resource objective, so its superiority in that objective over constrained responses follows by construction. This mathematical benchmark must not be presented as evidence of deployable price efficiency. Endogenous prices, unit commitment, network constraints, uncertain arrivals and imperfect forecasts remain to be incorporated in the empirical comparison. Prices derived from a realized coordinated optimum are retained only as an explicitly labelled oracle.

Analytic examples show that maximizing deliverable reduction can be welfare reducing even when a smaller event commitment is beneficial; equally cheap private responses can have opposite system effects; and forecast errors can reverse the sign of realized gains without any ex-post reoptimization. These examples justify correcting the comparison design, but they do not establish a general ranking between real-world price and event mechanisms.

Stage 21 integration note: frozen response trajectories now enter a fixed-capacity grid with commitment and transport constraints after independent task/power reconstruction. This is conditional perfect-foresight redispatch, not an online market experiment. Crucially, the two endpoints of the linear forecast objective are not bounds on nonlinear grid cost: a continuous follower-optimal set can have a strictly higher commitment cost at every interior point than at either extreme point. The constructive proof, integration scope and failure-state treatment are in `mechanism_grid_methods_draft.md`. General pessimistic grid-aware procurement is still unimplemented; no provincial mechanism ranking follows from these endpoint evaluations.
