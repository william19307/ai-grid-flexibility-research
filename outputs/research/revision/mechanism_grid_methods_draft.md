# Working methods: fixed responses and nonlinear grid costs

23 September 2026. Mathematical integration and counterexample; not completed provincial results.

For each frozen mechanism response, we reconstruct task work, release/deadline compliance, resource occupation, power including idle consumption, and service costs from the task allocations. The complete verified power trajectory enters the transport-network model as mandatory nodal demand. Installed generation, transmission and storage capacities, non-compute demand, initial states and the evaluation horizon are identical across responses. Capacity expansion and involuntary non-compute shedding are disabled. Unit commitment and dispatch are reoptimized conditional on the frozen task trajectory. Infeasible and unresolved solves remain distinct. Resource savings are calculated only when both the outside option and the evaluated response have optimal feasible dispatch solutions. Unfinished terminal minimum-time obligations are disclosed rather than treated as fulfilled.

This is full-horizon conditional redispatch with foresight, not operational evaluation under frozen day-ahead commitment or a demonstrated online information set. A finite-horizon cost comparison with unresolved terminal obligations is not a settled lifetime benefit. The separate accounting identity for supply cost, service cost, electricity bills and participation transfers remains unchanged. Procurement choices and task schedules are not reselected using realized grid outcomes.

**Proposition (failure of linear endpoint bounds under unit commitment).** The schedules minimizing and maximizing a linear forecast cost over a firm's cost-minimizing response set need not bound that set's grid supply costs when commitment is endogenous. Even evaluating every extreme point can miss the maximum.

**Construction and proof.** Consider one unit of divisible work within three unit-length intervals, unit active power, zero idle power, and tariff (0,1,1). The outside schedule is (1,0,0). A unit reduction commitment in the first interval restricts the follower cost-minimizing set to p(x)=(0,x,1−x), x∈[0,1], all with bill 1. A linear forecast vector (30,0,1) attains its extrema at x=1 and x=0. Let the physical grid have a supply option costing 5 per unit only in the first interval and two initially offline units, each available in one of the two recovery intervals, respectively. Each recovery unit has capacity 1, zero variable cost and startup cost 10; all minimum-time obligations are one interval. There is no alternative recovery supply or storage. At x=0 or x=1 exactly one startup is necessary and sufficient, giving cost 10. For every 0<x<1, positive demand occurs in both recovery intervals, so two startups are necessary and sufficient, giving cost 20. Hence both extreme-point costs are strictly below the maximum over the follower-optimal set. The outside schedule costs 5, yielding savings −5 at either endpoint and −15 throughout the interior. This establishes the result over the continuous response set; sampled numerical evaluations only check implementation.

The construction refutes using the stored linear-forecast endpoints as physical worst-case bounds. It is not an estimate of actual startup costs, forecast accuracy, provider behaviour or comparative market efficiency. For the example's offer set {0,1}, a separate grid-aware pessimistic comparison selects zero, whereas the frozen linear-forecast plan selects one; ex-post evaluation retains that original selection. General pessimistic procurement requires optimization over the full follower response set with grid costs, or an explicit implementable response-selection rule. The present integration implements neither a general bilevel mixed-integer solver nor an incentive-compatible contract.
