# Demand cohort accounting and non-linear capacity effects

This working draft describes the revised input component and its conditional checks, not completed empirical provincial validation.

Let Aᵖ denote the full electrical trajectory of a fixed cohort under policy p. If the supplied demand is a background B that excludes this cohort, total demand is Dᵖ = B + Aᵖ. If a supplied total L already contains the cohort under reference execution r, define the common background B = L − Aʳ and construct Dᵖ = B + Aᵖ. Reference reconstruction Dʳ = L, nonnegative B, identical nodal clocks and explicit power units are required. No negative residual is clipped and no policy trajectory is renormalized to the original energy total.

The background is passed to ordinary nodal demand, while cohort power is passed separately as mandatory demand. The no-cohort counterfactual removes only the declared electrical cohort. The verified gang-trace adapter defines that cohort as the entire replicated cluster, including untargeted GPU work and idle power; eligible jobs alone change policy. Removing this electrical cohort therefore removes more than the optimized jobs and does not represent removing all AI from the economy.

An accounting error that adds Aᵖ directly to an already inclusive L produces D̃ᵖ = Dᵖ + Aʳ. For a fixed unconstrained linear cost cᵀD, the added common trajectory cancels in policy cost differences. This invariance does not extend to peak or capacity costs. Consider L = (8,8), Aʳ = (4,0), and Aᵖ = (0,2) MW over two one-hour intervals. Correct accounting yields peaks 8 and 10 MW, whereas double counting yields peaks 12 and 10 MW. With a single expandable generator costing 10 currency units per MW and 5 per MWh, reference/alternative costs are 160/170 under correct accounting and 220/190 under double counting. Thus both the capacity direction and net-cost direction can reverse. This is an algebraic counterexample, not an estimate of the empirical study's bias.

The two-hour example was specified in commit d3af59d before implementation; all four solved capacities and costs match the analytic values. Twenty-five checks cover reconstruction, common backgrounds, mandatory cohort power, half-hour energy integration, complete nodal/time alignment, mutation detection, tied system peaks, and integration of four existing independently certified 192-hour gang trajectories. The integration uses a synthetic 3 MW background and assumed facility conversion (one replica, 0.5 kW full active power per GPU, idle ratio 0.41). It preserves the full recovery horizon and produces zero maximum dispatch balance residual against the analytic generator. Neither these assumptions nor the background are new measurements.

Content hashes protect consistency with the inspected inputs; they do not authenticate empirical scope. Provincial background demand, cohort coverage, measured power, service constraints and operational supply boundaries remain external evidence requirements before publishing new system-level benefits.
