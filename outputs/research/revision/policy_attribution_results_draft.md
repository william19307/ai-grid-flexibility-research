# Working results text: service-conditioned attribution

Status: a verified conditional replay result for the revision; not a final manuscript abstract or a provincial-system finding.

We compared four operational policies under identical jobs, resource constraints, background activity and retrospective information: observed starts at full speed, observed starts with variable execution modes, flexible starts at full speed, and flexible starts with variable modes. The 108 prespecified conditions reused 28,716 jobs from four clusters and crossed three power–throughput curves, three completion allowances and three relative tariff profiles. All 432 constructed schedules passed an independent event-level audit. These conditions are correlated sensitivity cases rather than independent experimental replications. The external power–throughput curves, homogeneous GPU power boundary and analyst-defined completion allowances are assumptions, not measurements of these jobs' hardware or contractual deadlines.

Attribution changed with the permitted service window. With no completion allowance beyond historical completion, the fixed-start variable-mode policy could not reduce execution speed. Across all 36 such conditions, deterministic bounds established that the start-time capability accounted for more than half of optimal joint bill savings. With allowances of 6 and 24 hours, the bounds instead established a majority contribution from mode choice in 34 and 33 of 36 conditions, respectively. Five conditions remained unidentified: their feasible-policy allocations exceeded one half, but their optimal-capability share intervals crossed that threshold. Feasible-policy allocations and bounded optimal-capability shares are distinct estimands. The intervals express optimization uncertainty and are not statistical confidence intervals.

Conditional savings in the total modeled GPU bill, including fixed background activity and idle power, ranged from 0.45–2.54%, 4.19–10.17% and 5.01–15.86% under the 0-, 6- and 24-hour allowances. Despite lower energy use in every tested condition, hourly GPU peak power increased in 57 of 108 conditions, by up to 11.37%. Neither the bill reductions nor the GPU peak changes establish whole-facility or electricity-system benefits. These results motivate an explicit separation between service permissions, facility power conversion, and grid valuation; they do not identify a universal physical division between energy efficiency and temporal flexibility.
